Platform Knowledge

Power Stroke, Duramax, Cummins

The big three light-truck diesels each have their own personality and their own favorite ways to fail. The work here starts from what your platform is actually known to do — not from a generic parts-swap script.

Ford Power Stroke

Ford's Power Stroke family has a well-earned reputation for oil cooler and EGR cooler headaches, injector trouble, and variable-geometry turbos that gum up and stick. Hard start when hot, sudden loss of power, coolant that keeps disappearing — it all gets pinned down with injector contribution tests, fuel and oil pressure readings, and boost data before any expensive part goes on the truck.

GM Duramax

From the early trucks' injector reputation to high-pressure fuel pump concerns on later ones, Duramax problems get expensive fast when somebody guesses. Balance rates, rail pressure behavior, turbo vane operation, and cooling system condition tell the real story on GM's 6.6L — so the repair path is set by evidence, not by a forum thread.

Ram Cummins

The 5.9 and 6.7 Cummins earn their reputation for going the distance, but miles still catch up: lift pumps, injectors, turbo actuators, and grid heater no-starts in cooler weather. White smoke, haze at idle, low power under load — each complaint gets tested and confirmed before the wrench comes out.

FAQ

Common Questions

What does diesel diagnosis include?

Scan data and stored codes first, then the tests that fit the complaint: injector balance rates, rail and fuel pressure, boost and turbo vane operation, and glow plug or grid heater checks for cold starts.

Can you help with hard-start issues?

Yes. A hard start when hot often points to injectors or fuel pressure; a hard start when cold points to glow plugs, the grid heater or compression. The pattern decides what gets tested first.

Do you work on warning light and drivability problems?

Yes. Limp mode, low power and warning lights often trace to a boost leak, a sticking turbo vane, EGR or DPF faults, or a sensor. Live data shows which one before parts are ordered.

Do you work on Power Stroke, Duramax, and Cummins trucks?

Yes. Ford, GM, and Ram light-truck diesels are the core of the diesel work here — diagnostics, injectors, turbo issues, cooling system repairs, and no-starts across all three platforms.

Why does testing matter so much before replacing diesel parts?

Because diesel parts are expensive. A set of injectors or a turbo costs real money, and swapping them on a hunch is how a repair bill doubles without fixing the truck. Testing confirms the failure before anything gets ordered.

Can you handle a diesel that cranks but won't start?

Yes. Diesel no-starts come down to fuel, air, compression, or electronics, and each gets checked in order — including glow plug and grid heater operation when cold starting is part of the complaint.
