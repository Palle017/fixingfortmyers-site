Stranded or exposed to traffic? Get appropriate roadside help first. If there is smoke, fire, a fuel smell or immediate danger, stop starting attempts and seek emergency assistance. Call 911 for an immediate emergency. Calling or texting Tony does not automatically dispatch help.

Why won’t my car start?

The sound, dashboard messages and recent history help choose the right checks. They do not identify a failed part by themselves. Here is how to describe the three main patterns.

Car won’t start and makes a clicking noise

Rapid clicks can occur with a low battery or poor electrical connection. One click can also involve the starter circuit. Note whether the lights dim and whether the engine turns at all. Working headlights do not prove there is enough power to start the engine.

Engine cranks but won’t start

“Cranking” means the engine turns over during the starting attempt. If it turns but never runs, the checks may involve fuel, ignition or engine controls. Tell Tony whether it only cranks, tries to catch, or starts briefly and stalls.

Lights come on, but there is no cranking

Power supply, starting controls and key recognition are possible areas to check. Include an exact message such as “key not detected.” Do not assume a new battery or starter will solve a silent no-start.

It still won’t start after a jump. Now what?

A failed jump-start does not prove the starter is bad. Likewise, a successful jump does not explain why the battery was low. Tell Tony who attempted it, what changed, and whether the engine stayed running. If this keeps happening after parking, read the recurring dead-battery guide.

Follow the vehicle’s owner’s manual and avoid repeated extended cranking. Do not attempt to jump a damaged or leaking battery, and do not use a generic jump-start procedure on an unfamiliar vehicle.

What does no-start diagnosis involve?

The starting point depends on which pattern you describe. Battery condition and electrical connections may need checking before the starter is judged. A vehicle that cranks normally may need a different sequence of tests. This is why identifying the symptom is more useful than requesting a replacement part from an online guess.

Ask Tony about the no-start diagnostic visit for your vehicle and location. He confirms access, availability and the next step before any dispatch.

What to text Tony if you’re stranded

  • Vehicle year, make and model, as much as you know.
  • “Rapid clicks,” “one click,” “cranks but won’t start,” or “nothing happens.”
  • Whether it stopped while driving or failed to start after parking.
  • Any dashboard message and whether a jump was already attempted.
  • City or ZIP, whether you are stranded, and whether the vehicle is clear of traffic.

You do not need a VIN, license plate or a diagnosis to ask for help. If the vehicle will not start and you are stranded, call Tony directly; you can also text the details.
