# Perfect Timing: 14-day local traffic test with $0 ad spend

Prepared October 9, 2026. This document contains ready-to-use drafts and an execution plan. It does not record publication, sent messages, account changes, new viewers, or booked work. The goal is qualified local repair inquiries and completed work; raw views are an early signal.

## Do these first

1. **Google Business Profile:** make the actual offered services easy to find, add real work photos, and publish one useful service post with a link to its matching page. Lead with the real mobile no-start photo, then follow with walnut blasting and engine/transmission work. Keep oil changes visible. Preserve the business name, categories, hidden address and owner-selected hours from the existing project decisions. AI intake at any hour is not a promise of immediate dispatch.
2. **Nextdoor:** use the existing business page for two useful local posts in week one and one follow-up in week two. The current Nextdoor product page says business posts are free and unlimited; older indexed articles saying twice a month are stale. Free posting does not guarantee citywide distribution. Use the audience actually offered in the account. [Current free Business Page offering](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/getting-started/business-page)
3. **Customer recommendations and referrals:** invite honest feedback from actual customers at normal job completion, and ask relevant existing contacts to share a service link with someone who needs it. No discount, prize or free service for a review. No filtering requests to only happy customers. This is an owner/customer conversation, not a mass-message campaign. [Google review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en)
4. **Five existing trade contacts:** prioritize a tow operator, a tire/alignment shop, a body shop, a detailer and a mechanic who does not take major engine/transmission work. Offer a clear referral path for jobs they decline. A genuine working relationship is more useful than hundreds of directory submissions.

The first recommendation follows Google's guidance that complete, accurate information, relevant services, photos and genuine reviews help customers understand a business. Local ranking also depends on distance and prominence; posts do not buy a ranking position. [Google local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) [Services editor](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9455399?hl=en)

## What counts as working

For each source, track **views/website clicks when available → received inquiries → reached and qualified → booked → completed → actual repair value**. A qualified inquiry is within the accepted service area, for work the shop offers, with a real vehicle problem and usable contact details. An oil change can qualify; do not judge prospects by assumptions about income.

| Field | How to record |
| --- | --- |
| Date and source | Google, Nextdoor, referral partner, community name, direct/unknown; retain the original source when known. |
| Post/service | Walnut, engine, transmission, oil change, no-start or other. Use the actual requested job, not the ad's headline. |
| Lead reference | Use the existing private intake reference; keep customer details out of public notes and campaign URLs. |
| Outcome | Received, reached, qualified, booked, completed, declined, pending. |
| Revenue and effort | Actual completed amount, quoted amount separately, and minutes spent on the channel. |

Google's profile “Calls” metric is a count of call-button clicks, not verified connected conversations. Compare it with the shop's received inquiries. [Google performance definitions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en)

Current repository evidence: `site.js` captures a simple `utm_source` into the browser session and attaches `channel` to a request. It does not by itself store `utm_campaign` or `utm_content`; existing visit events omit query strings. The links above identify the source today and retain conventional campaign labels for future reporting, but these extra labels alone do not create a working campaign dashboard. Verify the received channel in isolated test data before relying on it. Calls and direct texts still need “Where did you find us?” and honest unknown attribution.

At day 14, one suitable booked job is a useful reason to keep testing its source, not statistical proof that a channel wins. Plenty of views with no inquiries calls for a clearer message or better audience fit. Inquiries with poor booking rates call for review of response time, scope, diagnosis/estimate process and availability. Very low exposure is inconclusive. Extend a promising organic test before spending on ads; do not promise that money fixes an untested funnel.
