# Specialty-tool messaging proposal

Prepared September 24, 2026. Proposal only; no publication or website changes.

## Position and evidence

Lead with what the tools help Tony investigate, rather than how many he has. The owner's insight is that customers repeatedly choose Perfect Timing because Tony has access to a wide variety of specialty automotive tools. This is useful qualitative direction, not a measured retention ranking, customer testimonial, equipment inventory, or proof of tool ownership.

Reviewed the active worktree's homepage, diagnostics, electrical, no-start, A/C and module-programming pages, plus `docs/COMPANY-UPDATE.md` and `docs/GUIDES.md`. The current diagnostics copy already connects codes, live data and targeted checks; electrical explains circuit testing; A/C separates cooling from airflow concerns; programming asks for compatibility details before an appointment. Build on those explanations. The older no-start paragraph promises “the one actual cause” and “the only part you end up paying for”; do not reuse those unsupported outcome assurances in this campaign. No existing copy was edited.

The following are exactly three prioritized recommendations. All customer-facing copy is **drafted**, not owner-approved or published. Specific tools, procedures and case outcomes require Tony's confirmation before publication. Existing website claims are context, not independent verification of business capability.

## 1. Add a compact benefit section at the existing homepage contact point

**Why first / customer problem:** Lowest content burden and closest to an inquiry. Helps someone with an unresolved warning light, no-start or recurring fault understand why access to specialized tools may be relevant to their next repair decision.

**Exact placement:** In `index.html`, immediately after `section#repair-guides` and before `section#contact`. Use the existing section/container/button styles: one short paragraph, one genuine photo and one primary CTA. Keep the existing hero, navigation, service cards and contact form.

**Draft headline:** “Specialty tools. A clearer next step for your repair.”

**Draft body:** “Tony has access to a wide range of specialty automotive tools. The useful part is matching the equipment and testing to your vehicle's problem—then explaining what the findings mean. Tell us about the warning light, starting trouble or A/C concern, including any recent repairs. Tony will confirm whether he can help with your vehicle and location.”

**Draft CTA:** “Tell Tony what’s happening” → `/?service=diagnostics#contact`. Supporting line: “Prepare a text to (239) 397-2048, review it in your messaging app, then tap Send.” Retain existing email and copy alternatives.

**Evidence needed:** Tony's confirmation of the access statement, including any booking or location limitations; one actual photo of him using a relevant tool on a permitted job, with a plain caption naming the test and its purpose. An existing diagnostics asset is only a candidate until its provenance and pictured procedure are confirmed. No stock equipment wall or unverified brand list.

**Practical measure:** For four weeks after any later approved publication, manually count received inquiries that mention this section and contain vehicle details, symptoms and city/ZIP. Ask where customers found Tony; record confirmed appointments separately. Compare with an equal prior period if records exist. A CTA click or prepared SMS draft is an engagement signal, not a received lead; do not claim attribution or lift from small counts alone.

## 2. Show one real test in a short tool-in-use video

**Why second / customer problem:** Makes the benefit tangible for customers who think a code read already tells them what part to buy. Start with diagnostics, where the existing copy is strongest, and produce one 30–45 second captioned clip before committing to a series.

**Exact placement/channel:** Proposed video in `auto-diagnostics-fort-myers.html`, directly after “How The Work Gets Done,” with a text transcript and the existing contact link. Reuse the approved clip on an existing business social channel if the owner later authorizes posting; no account or Google profile changes are part of this task.

**Draft headline:** “What happens after we read the code?”

**Draft body / spoken opening:** “A trouble code gives us a starting point. It does not automatically tell us which part needs replacing. Here’s one test from a real job, what we were checking, and what the result helped us decide.” Follow with only documented case facts: customer symptom → actual tool/test → finding → next step or remaining uncertainty. Do not script a successful repair before a case exists.

**Draft CTA:** “Tell Tony your warning light and vehicle details” → `/?service=diagnostics#contact`. In a social caption, include “Text (239) 397-2048 with your year, make, model, symptoms and city.”

**Evidence needed:** Customer permission; actual equipment Tony can access; a readable, redacted test result; technician explanation of what it establishes and what it cannot establish; case notes and a recheck if a repair outcome is mentioned. Film the vehicle context, the tool in use and Tony's explanation. Hide customer names, plates, VINs and account screens. Tool category and brand stay unnamed until verified. A future electrical/no-start or A/C clip should meet the same evidence standard.

**Practical measure:** Record received texts/calls that identify the clip, how many fit Tony's confirmed scope, and how many become appointments over four weeks. Views and watch completion, if already available, help assess the opening; they do not establish repair inquiries. Do not install new tracking or add a provider for this pilot.

## 3. Add real testing examples to the existing symptom guides

**Why third / customer problem:** Reaches people trying to explain “car won’t start,” “battery keeps dying” or “A/C warm at idle.” These are high-intent topic hypotheses, not verified local search-volume or ranking claims. Existing guides reduce the need for new pages and keep useful URLs intact.

**Exact placement:** Add a short “What testing can help explain” section before the contact CTA in `repair-guide-car-wont-start.html`, `repair-guide-battery-keeps-dying.html` and `repair-guide-ac-warm-at-idle.html`, using the existing guide design. Start with whichever has the best documented case. Retain their electrical/electrical/ac contact selections and related-service links. Keep programming inquiries linked to the existing module-programming page; no broad vehicle-coverage promise or additional programming guide is needed for this pilot.

**Draft headline (A/C example):** “A/C warm at idle? Tell us when the cooling changes.”

**Draft body:** “A change between idling and driving is useful information, but it is not a diagnosis. A/C testing can include temperature, airflow and fan-operation checks. Tony's access to specialty tools can support the checks appropriate to your vehicle; he will confirm the work and equipment needed. Tell him whether the air gets warmer, the airflow gets weaker, or both—and whether the system has recently been serviced.”

**Draft CTA:** “Prepare an A/C text to Tony” → `/?service=ac#contact`. Preserve the explicit review-and-send instruction and email/copy fallbacks. For no-start and battery guides, use `/?service=electrical#contact` and ask whether it cranks/clicks/stays silent or how long it sits before going flat.

**Evidence needed:** One genuine case per enhanced guide: complaint, vehicle details, tool/test used, finding, repair or unresolved question, and any documented recheck. For A/C, confirm the pictured instrument and test conditions; never invent pressure readings or infer the failed component from idle behavior. For programming-related references, have Tony confirm the particular vehicle/module/procedure and access requirements before naming support. Access to a scan tool alone is not evidence of programming coverage.

**Practical measure:** Where already accessible, review search impressions/clicks for each guide and symptom query over six to eight weeks; otherwise leave search attribution unknown. Separately log received inquiries identifying the guide, qualification and booked appointments. No search position, lead volume or savings guarantee. A guide visit or draft opening cannot verify a customer sent anything.

## Tony's evidence checklist before publication

- List 3–5 tools he actually uses or can reliably access: purpose, owned/borrowed/rented/partner access, availability, mobile/site restrictions and confirmed vehicle/procedure limits. This is an internal inventory, not a public compatibility list.
- Choose one recent permitted case with symptom, test, finding, next step and documented recheck where available. Separate observed facts from interpretation; record unresolved issues.
- Capture one wide working photo, one tool-in-use detail and one redacted result. Record the actual test name and a customer-friendly sentence explaining why it mattered.
- Confirm any programming claim against the exact vehicle/module procedure and current access. Do not publish certification, dealership equivalence, brand endorsement or ownership claims without evidence.
- Have Tony review the final words and captions. The owner insight does not authorize invented customer quotes or “number one reason” language.
