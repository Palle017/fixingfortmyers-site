What To Watch For

Signs You Should Book

Common Symptoms

  • Engine revs flare between gears, or RPM climbs without the car speeding up
  • Hard, late or clunky shifts, especially into drive or reverse
  • A pause before the car moves after shifting into gear
  • Stuck in one gear with a warning light (limp mode)
  • Shudder at steady highway speed, often torque-converter related
  • Dark, burnt-smelling or low fluid, or a red puddle under the car

Why It Matters

A slipping transmission makes heat, and heat is what burns up clutches and breaks down fluid. Driving on a slip turns a repairable problem into a rebuild faster than almost anything else on the car. Plenty of shift complaints also have nothing to do with the transmission’s insides: low fluid, a bad speed sensor, a sticking solenoid or outdated control software can all feel like a failing transmission. Testing first tells you which one you have before the expensive answer gets quoted.

The Process

How The Work Gets Done

From Symptom to Answer

  1. Check fluid level and condition the way the manufacturer specifies; many sealed units only read correctly at operating temperature
  2. Scan the transmission codes and freeze-frame data, not just the engine codes
  3. Road test while watching live data: gear commanded versus actual, slip, and torque-converter lockup
  4. Test the shift and pressure-control solenoids, speed sensors and wiring before anything comes apart
  5. Check for software updates or relearn procedures that apply to your transmission
  6. If the testing points inside, the transmission comes out in the workshop and gets torn down and inspected
  7. You get repair, rebuild and replacement options quoted in writing, and you decide

Real-World Example

What This Usually Looks Like

A customer calls sure the transmission is finished because it flares between second and third. The first hour goes to fluid level, codes and live data. Sometimes that is where it ends: low fluid from a leaking cooler line, or a shift solenoid that tests bad. Sometimes the data shows real clutch slip, and then the transmission comes out and gets opened up in the workshop before anyone talks numbers. Either way the quote follows the testing, not the other way around.

FAQ

Questions We Hear A Lot

Can I keep driving a slipping transmission?

Not far, and not hard. Slipping makes heat, and heat breaks down the fluid and burns the clutches, which makes the slip worse. If it slips, flares or will not engage, park it and call. If it only shifts a little firm, it is usually fine to drive it in for diagnosis.

Will a fluid change or an additive fix slipping?

If the fluid is low, bringing it to the correct level can fix it, and that is the first thing checked. A fluid change on a transmission that is already slipping rarely cures it, and additives can hide symptoms without fixing worn parts. Tony checks the fluid level and condition before recommending anything.

Rebuild, remanufactured or used: which is better?

It depends on what failed, the vehicle, and how long you plan to keep it. A rebuild fixes what is worn in the unit you have. A remanufactured unit is rebuilt to a standard by a supplier and comes with its own coverage. A used unit is usually the fastest and cheapest, and the biggest gamble. Tony quotes the options that make sense for your vehicle in writing.

How long does transmission diagnosis take?

Many problems are narrowed down with a scan, a fluid check and a road test in part of a day. Intermittent problems, or ones that only show up hot, can take longer because the condition has to be reproduced. Teardown and inspection, if it is needed, is scheduled with you.

What should I tell Tony when I book?

The year, make, model and mileage; what it does and when (cold, hot, a certain gear, under load); any warning lights; and whether it still drives. A short video of the shift or the dash lights helps, and you can send one with your request.
