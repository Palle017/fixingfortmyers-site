General vehicle information · Updated September 24, 2026

Flashing light, shaking or loss of power? Pull over when safe, stop using the vehicle and arrange roadside assistance. Do not keep driving to see whether the light clears. If there is smoke, fire, a fuel smell or immediate danger, seek emergency help first.

What does a flashing check engine light mean?

A flashing light can accompany an engine misfire, where combustion is not occurring as expected. Continued operation can damage other components, including the catalytic converter. It does not tell you which part caused the problem.

Describe whether the car shook at idle, lost power or ran roughly before you stopped. Do not drive or rev the engine to recreate the symptom. If the light flashed and then stopped, that history still matters; a symptom disappearing is not proof the fault is repaired.

What if the check engine light is steady?

A steady light also needs diagnosis, even when the vehicle seems to run normally. The warning can have different causes and severities. Check the owner’s manual for your exact warning; an oil-pressure or temperature warning is a different situation.

A webpage cannot clear a vehicle for driving. Mention any rough running, overheating, unusual noise or loss of power when you call. If the car’s operation changes or the light starts flashing, stop when safe and get help.

I have a trouble code. Do I already know the repair?

A diagnostic trouble code is a clue to the affected system or condition. It is not a parts order. A code description may point toward a component even when its wiring, connection or another condition needs attention.

If a shop or parts store has already scanned the car, send the exact code and any test report you have. You do not need to buy a reader before contacting the mechanic. The check-engine diagnostic service connects the code, symptoms and test results before a repair is recommended.

Can the mechanic tell me how long the repair will take?

The first useful step is identifying the fault. Once the needed work, parts and access are understood, the mechanic can discuss timing. A flashing light by itself cannot establish the repair cost or support a same-day promise.

Mention if the vehicle is your only transportation or if you have a deadline. That helps frame the conversation about the next step without turning an initial inquiry into a confirmed appointment.

What to text about a warning light

  • Vehicle year, make, model and city or ZIP.
  • Whether the light is steady, flashing, or flashed earlier.
  • Shaking, rough idle, power loss or other changes you noticed.
  • Any exact code, recent repair or event before the light appeared.
  • Whether the vehicle is now parked and the engine is off.

No code or photo is required to ask for help. If the engine no longer starts, include that and see the no-start guide. The mechanic confirms the diagnostic plan, shop appointment and availability.
