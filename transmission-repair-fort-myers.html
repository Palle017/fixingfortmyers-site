What To Watch For

Tell us what happens between gears

Common Symptoms

  • Delayed engagement or slipping
  • Hard shifts, flare, or gear hunting
  • Transmission warning lights or codes
  • Fluid leaks or burnt-smell fluid
  • Drivability complaints that seem transmission-related

Why It Matters

A shifting problem can involve fluid, controls or internal wear. The symptoms alone do not tell you which. If the vehicle loses drive, slips badly or will not engage reliably, stop driving and call to discuss transport. Continuing to drive can make the damage worse.

Worth Knowing

What Matters Most

Before you call, note whether the problem happens cold, after warming up, in a particular gear or while slowing down. Include the mileage and any recent fluid service or repair. A short description of when it happens is more useful than guessing which part failed.

FAQ

Common Questions

Can engine or electrical issues feel like transmission trouble?

Yes. Misfires, sensor faults or voltage problems can affect how a vehicle drives and shifts. Testing helps separate those issues from an internal transmission fault.

Can you discuss a rebuild or replacement?

Yes. We review the findings and vehicle details before discussing the available repair, rebuild or replacement route. The right option depends on the transmission, extent of damage and availability.

Should I book a fluid change for a slipping transmission?

Describe the slipping when you call. A fluid service is not a diagnosis or a repair for worn internal parts. The level, condition, leaks and operating data may need to be checked before deciding what work is appropriate.

What if the car will not move?

Do not keep revving it to force it into gear. Call or text the shop with your vehicle details and location to discuss availability and transport to the workshop. Ask about pickup before making arrangements.
