Why it happens

Fuel no longer washes the valves

The problem

On engines with direct injection only, fuel sprays into the cylinder instead of washing the backs of the intake valves. Oil vapor can contribute to carbon deposits that interfere with airflow and engine operation. A fuel-tank additive does not reach those valve backs. Engines with both port and direct injection have a different cleaning effect from fuel.

Why it matters

Heavy deposits can contribute to rough running, hesitation and misfires. When carbon is the cause, cleaning removes those deposits and can restore lost performance. Ignition, fuel and mechanical faults can cause similar symptoms, so testing comes first.

The process

Test first, then clean

How the job is done

  1. Confirm the cause with codes, live data and misfire tests. Ignition, fuel and mechanical faults can look the same.
  2. Remove the intake to reach the valves.
  3. With the valves for the port fully closed, clean using walnut-shell media and suction to collect debris, following the engine-specific procedure.
  4. Reassemble, clear the codes and check how the engine runs.

Common questions

Will it fix my misfire?

Only if carbon is causing it. That is why testing comes first.

Is every engine a candidate?

No. It is mainly for direct-injection gasoline engines. Port-injected engines rarely build up this kind of carbon. Send the engine size if you know it.
