Intake carbon cleaning · Fort Myers, FL

Walnut Blasting in Fort Myers

Rough idle, a misfire that keeps coming back, or power that has faded over the miles? On direct-injection engines, carbon on the intake valves is a common cause. Tony tests to confirm it, then blasts the valves clean with crushed walnut shell.

Signs worth checking

  • Rough or shaky idle, often worse when cold
  • Misfire codes that return after plugs or coils
  • Hesitation or lost power under load
  • Higher mileage on a direct-injection engine

Why it happens

Fuel no longer washes the valves

The problem

A direct-injection engine sprays fuel straight into the cylinder, so nothing rinses the backs of the intake valves. Oil vapor from the crankcase bakes on as hard carbon. Over time it chokes the airflow into each cylinder and upsets the idle and mixture. Fuel additives can’t reach it.

Why it matters

Left alone, heavy deposits keep causing misfires, and repeated misfires are hard on plugs, coils and the catalytic converter. Replacing those parts won’t fix a carbon problem. Cleaning the valves removes the cause.

The process

Test first, then clean

How the job is done

  1. Confirm the cause with codes, live data and misfire tests. Ignition, fuel and mechanical faults can look the same.
  2. Remove the intake to reach the valves.
  3. Blast each port with walnut-shell media under suction, so the debris comes out with the carbon.
  4. Reassemble, clear the codes and check how the engine runs.

What you get

  • An answer on whether carbon is the cause before you pay for cleaning
  • The work explained and approved before it starts
  • Clean intake ports and restored airflow when carbon is the problem
  • A straight answer if testing points somewhere else

Common questions

Will it fix my misfire?

Only if carbon is causing it. That is why testing comes first.

Is every engine a candidate?

No. It is mainly for direct-injection gasoline engines. Port-injected engines rarely build up this kind of carbon. Send the engine size if you know it.

Already told you need a carbon clean?
