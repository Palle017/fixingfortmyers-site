Common Concerns

  • Vehicle exposed to flooding, storm surge or standing water
  • Wet carpet, cabin moisture or a leak around seals
  • Electrical faults and warning lights after water exposure
  • Corrosion affecting wiring, connectors or modules
  • Questions about a flood-damaged car before purchase or repair

Safety Comes First

Freshwater and saltwater can damage engines, wiring, control modules and safety equipment. Water depth, duration and hidden corrosion matter even after an interior seems dry.

If water may have reached the engine or electrical systems, do not start or drive the vehicle. Arrange safe transport and request an assessment before attempting repairs.

How it works

Decide Whether Restoration Is Practical

Step by Step

  1. Send the vehicle details, photos and flood history.
  2. Confirm an inspection scope, location and appointment.
  3. Evaluate accessible signs of water intrusion, corrosion and electrical concerns as agreed.
  4. Discuss safety, repair feasibility and any specialist referral needs.
  5. Proceed only after proposed work and estimates are approved.

Some Cars Should Not Be Repaired

Deep or saltwater flooding and safety-system damage can make a car unsafe or uneconomical to restore. An inspection does not guarantee that drying or replacing parts will make the vehicle safe, and specialist remediation or an insurer's total-loss process may be more appropriate.

FAQ

Vehicle Flood Damage Questions

Should I start a car that was flooded?

If water may have entered the engine or electrical systems, do not attempt to start or drive it. Arrange safe transport and request an assessment.

Can every flood-damaged car be restored?

No. Flood depth, duration, saltwater exposure, corrosion, damage to safety systems and repair cost determine whether restoration is viable.

What details should I send?

Share the vehicle year, make and model, whether the water was fresh or saltwater, the waterline, exposure date and photos if available.
