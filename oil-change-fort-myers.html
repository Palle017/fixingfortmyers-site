When You're Due

Due for service? Bring up the other concerns, too.

Common Signals

  • Oil life monitor or maintenance reminder is on
  • You're past the mileage or months listed in your owner's manual
  • You do not know when the oil and filter were last changed
  • You have noticed leaks, oil use or a new noise to discuss
  • Your driving conditions may call for the manufacturer’s severe-service schedule

Why It Matters

An oil change is also a useful time to bring up the things you have been putting off: that drip in the driveway, a brake noise or the light on the dash. Tell us at booking. Visible concerns can be noted during service; a separate diagnostic inspection may be needed to find the cause.

The Process

How An Oil Service Gets Done

What the Service Includes

  1. Drain the old oil and note any visible concerns
  2. Replace the oil filter and check the drain plug and gasket
  3. Fill with the correct viscosity and spec — conventional or full synthetic, per your manufacturer
  4. Look over belts, hoses, fluid levels, battery terminals, and tires while the vehicle is up, and check underneath for leaks
  5. Reset the oil life monitor and flag anything worth watching before it becomes a repair

What to mention when you book

  • Year, make, model and approximate mileage
  • Any oil use, leaks, noises or warning lights
  • Other maintenance or repairs you already know it needs
  • When you need the car back, so scheduling can be discussed

Choosing Your Oil

The right oil and interval for your engine

Start with the oil specification

The viscosity on the bottle is only part of the requirement. Your engine may also specify a particular approval or performance standard. We check the vehicle’s requirements when selecting oil and a filter.

Then check how you use the car

Follow the manufacturer’s time, mileage and oil-life guidance for your vehicle. Short trips, extended idling and towing may affect the schedule. Ask us to review your driving conditions rather than picking the same interval for every car.

FAQ

Common Questions

Can I book just an oil change?

Yes. Call or text the shop with your vehicle details to arrange oil and filter service. Appointments are confirmed directly.

Can you check other problems at the same visit?

Tell us about them when booking so the visit can be planned. A visible check may identify a concern, while noises, warning lights or drivability issues may require separate diagnostic work. Any additional repair is discussed before approval.

How often should I change my oil?

Use the manufacturer’s schedule and oil-life guidance for your exact vehicle and driving conditions. Time matters as well as mileage. If the service history is uncertain, mention it when you book.

Does an oil change fix low oil pressure or engine noise?

Those symptoms need evaluation. If an oil-pressure warning appears while the engine is running, stop safely and shut the engine off. Call to discuss the problem before driving to the appointment.

Do you reset the oil-life reminder?

The service reminder is reset as applicable after the oil service. Continue checking the oil level as directed in the owner’s manual between changes.
